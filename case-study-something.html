00 / The story

A house built from two letters.

Brief
Make the S and the P feel like home.

Something Property landed on my desk during my in-house years at Lime Marketing — a South African real estate firm with a small team, a big ambition, and a name that begged for a clever mark. The directive was straightforward: a logo and a full corporate stationery suite that could hold its own in a boardroom and on a roadside A-frame.

The first sketches chased the obvious — a roof, a key, a door — until the letters did the work themselves. The S, drawn with a squared shoulder, became the pitched silhouette of a house. The P slipped in behind it, gold against black, peeking out like a second occupant under the same roof.

Two letters, one home. The S and the P live inside the house shape together — security, warmth, unity for whoever moves into a property this firm sells. Not just a building. A home.

The palette followed the logic: estate black for the gravitas of title deeds and signed contracts; property gold for the value, the warmth, the small luxury of finally getting the keys. Poppins anchored the wordmark; Inter handled the paperwork. The whole system rolled out across business cards, letterheads, apparel, presentation folders and street-side A-frames — every touchpoint a client meets between the first viewing and the final handover.

05 / Approach

Two letters, one home.

01

Symbol

A house silhouette built from the letter S, with a gold P tucked behind it. Two letters, one home — the brand promise compressed into a single mark.

02

Palette

Black for the estate-agent gravitas, gold for the value and care, ivory for the paperwork. A palette that prints clean on a folder and reads from across the street.

03

System

Primary, horizontal, stacked, icon, app-icon — five lockups so the mark holds its weight on a 16px favicon and a 6-metre signboard.

04

Apply

Rolled across business cards, letterhead, branded apparel, A-frame signage, presentation folders and a directional sign at the property — the full handover stack.
